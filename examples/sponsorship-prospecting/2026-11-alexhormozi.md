## 4. Caveats

- Audience and monetization fields are model estimates; confirm with the creator's own analytics before contracting. Untapped means no sponsor detected in the last parse, not never sponsored.
- Query drift under filters: with the audience filters and the subscriber band applied, the semantic query lost most of its relevance. The audience-filtered untapped pass returned 20 hits with 2 on topic (van life, football, Warhammer and off-grid channels filled the rest) and the proven pass returned 11 with none on topic. Rerunning both without the audience filters returned 40 and 23 matches with roughly a third on topic. The earlier niche-finder example documented about a third off-topic on the app's default view; with four or more restrictive filters the drift is worse. The workaround used here is the one in the skill's step 6: search with fewer filters and apply the audience test in hand. This cost 10 credits over the skill's 10 to 15 budget.
- The list stops at 14 usable prospects, not 30: the index returned fewer than 15 on-topic channels in the tier across four searches. A third angle ("accounting software for freelancers") would likely add more; it was not run to stay near budget.
- Fit C channels came from a `search_related_outliers` hit in the sibling competitor-landscape run, which does not carry audience fields; their audience is inferred and marked as such.
- Credits measured across this run and the two sibling runs (competitor-landscape, monetization-research) in one session: balance 884 to 805, 79 credits, against 42 at the list prices in the data-layer skill (five `search_channels`, two `search_related_channels` and one `search_related_outliers` counted once each). The measured cost is almost double the documented list price; the data-layer skill's cost table should be rechecked against the billing rules before the examples' "credits used" lines are trusted.
- Not checked: brand safety of comments, rates, whether any channel is under an exclusive deal.
