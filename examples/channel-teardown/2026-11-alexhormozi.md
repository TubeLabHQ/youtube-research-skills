**TL;DR. The channel's own data says the audience comes for identity-call advice ("if you're ambitious but inconsistent") and leaves when the title names a business segment or promises a consulting session with a stranger. Sixteen "Building a $X Business for a Stranger" style uploads sit at a median ratio of 0.47, while the eight identity-call titles in the sample average above 2.**

1. Retire the "Building a $[amount] Business for a Stranger in [N] Minutes" packaging. Keep the consulting format, but package every episode as a stake or a question the way "Can I Save This Failing Theme Park in 90 Days?" did (1,800,000 views, ratio 3.71, the best video of the last six months).
2. Return the identity-call title to the monthly slate. The four best titles in the sample all address the viewer directly: "If you're ambitious but inconsistent, please watch this" (2,000,000, 4.12), "If I Started A Business in 2026, I'd Do This" (2,000,000, 4.12), "Brutally Honest Truths That Give You an Unfair Advantage in Life" (1,700,000, 3.51), "If I Wanted to Make My First $100K in 2026, I'd Do This" (1,600,000, 3.30).
3. Stop naming the segment in the title. "Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale" (131,000, 0.27), "Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes" (102,000, 0.21) and "Watch This If You Have a Service Business" (360,000, 0.74) all shrink the audience to the segment named. The same content with a universal frame, "How I Would Build a $10M Service Business (If I Had to Start Over)", did 1,400,000 at 2.89.
4. Drop the runtime-in-title device. Eleven titles carry "in N minutes", "give me N minutes" or "N years"; the ones without a stake underperform ("Learn 97% of How to Get Rich in 11 Mins" 92,000, 0.19; "give me 14 minutes and you'll learn how to be absurdly happy with your life" 228,000, 0.47).
5. Follow up the theme park. Comments on that episode report the park closed a month after publishing; a "what happened after" episode is a stake the audience already cares about and the only sequel the data supports.

One video hit 2,000,000 views at 4.12 times the channel's average. Sixteen videos in the same twelve months, the consulting sessions, averaged 228,500 at a median ratio of 0.47, under the channel's own median of 334,000. Same host, same subscribers, same month in several cases. The difference is entirely in the packaging.

The decay is visible run over run. "Building a $5,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 42 Mins" did 435,000 (0.90). Then "Building a $3,500,000 Business for a Stranger in 51 Mins" 384,000 (0.79), "Building a $3,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 57 Mins" 269,000 (0.55), "Building a $3,500,000 Business for Two Strangers in 52 Minutes" 211,000 (0.44), "Building a $2,300,000/yr Business for a Stranger in 57 Mins" 224,000 (0.46). A 69-minute edition recovered to 588,000 (1.21), then the slide resumed: "Building a $12,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 25 Minutes" 194,000 (0.40), "Building a $1,000,000 Business for a Stranger in 26 Minutes" 162,000 (0.33), "Helping 6 Business Owners Scale in 33 Minutes" 97,000 (0.20). The rebrand to "Scale or Fail" did not fix it: "$100M Worth of Marketing Knowledge in 32 Minutes | Scale or Fail" did 165,000 (0.34) in September. First use to last is a 2.6x drop; first use to the worst edition is 4.5x. The one episode in this family that worked changed the promise and kept the mechanism: "Can I Save This Failing Theme Park in 90 Days?" is the same consulting format with a stake, a deadline and a question, and it did 1,800,000 at 3.71.

**Promise rung.** The last ten titles sit at a median of rung 2: "Change My Mind, Win $10,000" (707,000, 1.46), "8 Entrepreneurs Compete For $100,000" (784,000, 1.62) and "How to become rich with social media (my exact playbook)" (601,000, 1.24) are promise plus constraint; "If I could start over in business, I'd do this sooner" (176,000, 0.36) is a bare promise. The channel's positive outliers sit at rung 4, where the mechanism carries everything: "If you're ambitious but inconsistent, please watch this" says nothing about the content and did 2,000,000. The niche's current breakouts are at rung 3 or 4 too. Chris Koerner (@thekoerneroffice, 724,000 subscribers) "The Most Overlooked $1M Business Every Town Needs" did 1,087,782 at 7.34; Vanader (@Vanader, 339,000) "This $531 Warehouse Setup Produces $1,000,000+ a Year" did 1,418,996 at 13.48; Ramit Sethi (@ramitsethi, 1,140,000) "We have $11M in assets. Why are we still living month to month?" did 1,252,903 at 6.91. Each names a specific mechanism or a specific contradiction. A Hormozi title at rung 2 is a gap of two rungs against where the audience is clicking.

**Authority signal.** This channel does not need a credential in the title; the name is the credential, which is why "How to Change Your Life" (702,000, 1.45) works here and would die elsewhere. What fails is the opposite problem: titles that spend the authority on a narrow audience. "Helping E-Commerce Business Owners Scale" tells four million subscribers that this one is not for them, and 131,000 (0.27) is the result. Valuetainment (@VALUETAINMENT, 7,160,000) shows the other way to use a big name: "Diesel Prices Are Out of Control: Here Is What Comes Next" did 1,273,791 at 6.12 by pointing the authority at something everyone feels this week. The fix principle is universal frame, specific mechanism: the audience is named by identity ("ambitious", "in your 20s or 30s"), the content by a number or a named move.

**Exhausted formats.** Beyond the consulting series, the runtime device is tiring. "It took me 36 years to realize what I'll tell you in 26 minutes…" did 251,000 (0.52) in March; "It took me 37 years to realize what I'll tell you in 18 minutes." did 689,000 (1.42) in September, so the device still works when the content behind it is a life lesson, and fails when it fronts a business segment ("Helping 4 Educational Business Owners Build a $1M Business in 25 Minutes", 102,000, 0.21). Reaction and ranking formats are dead on this channel: "Reacting to My First Videos 10 Years Later" 69,770 (0.14), "I Ranked the Best Superbowl Ads 2026 [TIER LIST]" 96,000 (0.20), "Breaking down Rihanna's Company (Real Tactics You Can Use)" 107,000 (0.22), "STEAL Ryan Reynolds' $1.3B Marketing Strategy [NO FAME REQUIRED]" 116,000 (0.24). The audience does not come here for commentary on other people's businesses.

Strategically the data supports three pillars. First, identity-call lessons: the eight titles that address "you" by situation rather than by segment are the channel's whole top tier, and the comments under them quote lines back ("You suck for a long time, until you suck so little that you're actually good"). Second, "if I started over" playbooks, which at their best hit 2,000,000 (4.12) and 1,600,000 (3.30) and at their worst still clear the median when the frame is universal. Third, consulting with stakes, one episode a month, packaged as a question with a deadline, never as a dollar figure and a runtime. Packaging rules for a channel this size: titles under 60 characters, no segment names, one number or one named mechanism, no runtime. Cadence in the sample is 5 to 8 uploads a month (October 7, September 8, August 5); the mix that the ratios reward is roughly two lessons, one playbook, one stakes episode, and nothing in the reaction or ranking family. Positioning: the only operator at this scale who publishes the playbook and then runs it on camera with a real business and a real deadline.

Title templates that keep the promise novel across reuses: "If you're [situation], please watch this" (example: "If you're good at your job but bad at money, please watch this"). "Can I [verb] This [failing thing] in [N] Days?" (example: "Can I Fix This Dying Gym in 60 Days?"). "The Overlooked [$ figure] Business Every [place] Needs" (example: "The Overlooked $2M Business Every Suburb Needs"). "I'm [age]. Here's What I Wish I Knew at [younger age]" (example: "I'm 37. Here's What I Wish I Knew at 23"). "How Everything Changes After [$ threshold]" (example: "How Everything Changes After Your First $100K").

Root cause in one sentence: the channel packages a third of its output by what Alex is doing and the audience clicks on what they are. The unfair advantage is the consulting-with-stakes format, proven once at 1,800,000 and never repeated. The single thing to change first is the title of the next consulting episode: a question with a deadline, not a dollar figure with a runtime.
